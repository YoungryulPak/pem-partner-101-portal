PEM and the four perspectives

Every perspective starts from Preemptive exposure management (PEM). A real customer has more than one kind of exposure. The same six-process lifecycle runs from four angles. Each angle has a natural solution.

The four perspectives

PerspectiveQuestionSolution
TPCRM: third-party cyber risk management (often shortened to TPRM)Which suppliers, and their suppliers, put me at risk?SecurityScorecard
Own outside-inWhat can an attacker see and reach from the internet about my own organization?SecurityScorecard
Inside-outWhat is wrong inside my infrastructure, applications, identities, code and AI tools?Mondoo
Pen-test validationCan an attacker really exploit it, and did the fix work?Alt Security

The two outside-in views are delivered by the same vendor. They aim at different targets. One looks at other companies. The other looks at your own company. Pen-test validation does not find exposure on its own. It proves what the other three perspectives found.

The same six processes in each perspective

Why one vendor is rarely enough

Gartner expects the market to move to unified platforms. Discovery, validation and remediation need to work as one loop. Each solution here is strong in some processes and partial in others. The governance layer joins the four perspectives together. It gives one risk view, one autonomy policy and one measure, MTTN. See Reference architecture and Closed-loop operating flow.

Mapping to CTEM

CTEM is the program structure PEM runs inside. Crossing its five stages with the four perspectives gives the same picture in CTEM terms. See CTEM: the program method.
