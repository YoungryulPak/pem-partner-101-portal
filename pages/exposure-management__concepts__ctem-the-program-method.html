CTEM: the program method

Continuous Threat Exposure Management (CTEM) is a program structure. A team uses it to run exposure management. PEM is the operating model. PEM does the work at machine speed. CTEM says what stages a program goes through. PEM says how much of each stage to automate. PEM also says how people supervise it.

#CTEM stageWhat happens
1ScopingDecide which business services, assets and suppliers matter most
2DiscoveryFind assets, exposures, misconfigurations and risky relationships in scope
3PrioritizationRank exposures by exploitability, threat activity and business impact
4ValidationConfirm which exposures an attacker can actually use
5MobilizationGet the right owners to fix, with approval and tracking

How CTEM maps to the six PEM processes

CTEM stagePEM processes
Scoping and DiscoveryDiscover
Discovery and PrioritizationAssess, Prioritize
ValidationValidate
MobilizationRemediate, Mitigate

What CTEM adds to vulnerability management

  • It is continuous. It is not a quarterly scan.
  • It covers more than CVEs. It also covers configuration, identity, code, SaaS, AI and third parties.
  • It includes validation. Effort goes only to exposures that matter.
  • It ends in action. Every exposure gets an owner and tracking, not just a line in a report.

What PEM adds to CTEM

CTEM does not say how much of the cycle to automate. It also does not say how fast the cycle must run. PEM answers both questions. PEM automates every stage, including the fix, inside an autonomy policy. PEM then measures the result as MTTN.
